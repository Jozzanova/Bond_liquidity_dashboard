# Liquidity letter, 5 November 2026

This is the first letter, written from the data on the page as of 4 November. The headline is the US Treasury market: dealer-to-customer turnover in off-the-run coupons ran at about USD 152bn per trading day over the twelve months to October 2026, and the October figure of 155bn is 34 % above October 2025. Turnover in every market with monthly data is well above a year earlier; the question for the coming months is whether the spring peak was the high-water mark.

## Turnover by market

In US Treasuries, all-venue turnover in nominal coupons averaged USD 950bn per day over the window and reached 1,031bn in October, down 5.6 % on September but 33 % higher than a year earlier. The peak month in the series was March 2026 at 1,203bn per day. Off-the-run dealer-to-customer turnover, the segment the liquidity profile sells into by default, follows the same shape: 174bn per day in March, 155bn in October.

Japanese government bonds show the same pattern a few months earlier. Customer turnover in the twelve months to September 2026 averaged JPY 3.7tn per day, and the September figure of 5.2tn per day is 17 % above September 2025, though 2.8 % below August. The series peak was April 2025 at 6.2tn per day for customers and March 2026 at 9.4tn for customers plus half of dealer turnover.

{figure: JPY d2c_base}

UK gilts, with quarterly data to the second quarter of 2026, averaged GBP 43bn per day across customer and professional counterparties, of which 18.6bn customer. German Bunds are an annual figure: EUR 21bn per day in 2025 including half of the broker legs, 17.6bn customer-facing, with the French OAT level derived from it at EUR 13.6bn. Chinese government bonds traded CNY 409bn per day in the onshore interbank market over the year to September 2026, of which foreign investors accounted for 18.7bn through Bond Connect and the other channels.

## Dealer-to-customer against interdealer

The US market remains the one where the interdealer leg is larger than the customer leg: USD 547bn per day on ATS and interdealer venues against 403bn dealer-to-customer across all issues, a 58 to 42 split. In gilts the professional side is also the larger at 24.5bn against 18.6bn. In JGBs customers dominate, 3.7tn against 2.3tn for the halved dealer leg, and in Bunds the customer-facing legs are 17.6bn against 3.8bn of halved broker legs. These ratios have been stable over the windows; the growth in the past year has come from both legs.

## Along the curve

Short maturities carry most of the turnover everywhere. In US Treasuries the 0-3y bucket is 58bn of the 152bn off-the-run dealer-to-customer total, with 10y+ second at 35bn; in gilts the long end leads, 7.7bn of the 18.6bn customer total in 10y+, which is the segment a long-duration holding sells into. JGB customer turnover is split 1.6tn, 1.1tn and 1.0tn across 0-5y, 5-10y and 10y+.
